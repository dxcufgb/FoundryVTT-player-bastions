# Changelog

## 0.2.0 (unreleased)
- Character sheets: the Bastion lives in the dnd5e character sheet's own Bastion tab, as if it were part of the system. Under the Bastion's name, a row of sub-tabs (Overview, Stats, Defense, Facilities, Projects, Dispatches, Trade, History) holds the whole Bastion page; Facilities is the new Facilities page (below), with the system's description under it. A button pops the page out into its own window. Owners get the tab at any level and even with the system's Bastion rules off. The system's name field shows the Bastion's name (the GM can rename it there). The extra rook button in the character sheet's header is gone; the group sheet keeps one.
- Facilities page, the same in the Bastion window and the character sheet: the defenders as a list by kind (guards, mercenaries, the facilities' defenders with their portraits, War Room lieutenants, engine crews), each with its count and limit, then a section per kind of building (Living quarters, Fortifications, Food and water, Access and travel, Trade and crafting, Staff and prestige, Communication and arcane). Each section starts with a summary of what the Bastion has and its current limit (beds, the wall's tier, food stores, the road, the best merchants, hired crews, ways to send dispatches), then its buildings as rows like the defenders' (picture, name, what it is, buttons, a chevron to open it): the Bastion's own and the dnd5e facilities on every owner's sheet, sorted into the same sections, with buildings under way greyed and their progress. A double arrow up marks a building that can be upgraded (the next wall tier, a bigger room, the next road step, a revenue tier, a facility that can be enlarged). A section's Plan button opens the catalog on that section. The top line shows the room on the grounds and each owner's special facility slots. In the character sheet the page replaces the system's defenders and facility lists; the system's description stays under it.
- Bastion Codex: a compendium ("Bastion Codex") with a page for every building the module builds (93) and every kind of inhabitant (guards, mercenaries, the facilities' defenders, War Room lieutenants, engine crews, servants, hirelings, building crews, refugees, and one generic page each for merchants, guests and travellers). The pages are the module's own journal page types (Facility, Inhabitant), in journals by section, each with a painting, an icon, what the place or the people are, and a table of what they add to and take from the Bastion, read live from the catalog and the house rules (cost, time, room, upkeep, effects, tiers, needs). 106 paintings made with ElevenLabs in the style of the Bastion's art.
- Facilities page: every row of the module's own shows its Codex icon; clicking a building or a kind of defender opens its Codex page (the upgrade arrow still opens the Projects page, at that building, highlighted). Each section has stat columns (beds, defense, stores, income, upkeep and so on: what each building or defender adds or takes) with a total row.
- Trade routes: contracts with other settlements, always both ways: the Bastion sends goods and is paid in gold every turn (timber, cloth, wine, ore, spices), or the settlement sends food every day (grain, livestock: 30 a day from a village, 48 from a town, 75 from a city) and the Bastion pays for it in gold, at 60% of the price of food bought when the stores run dry (paid as upkeep; a raided delivery isn't paid for). Trade routes have a tab of their own on the Bastion page, Trade Routes, next to Trade, with placeholder icons for each size of settlement (hamlet to metropolis, a signpost when the size isn't known). The GM keeps a list of settlements per Bastion there (name, distance in miles, size); those, the Bastion's own settlement and map notes within 150 miles of its marker can ask at a turn, or the GM makes one ask, and the owners can seek a route with any of them themselves (they pick the goods; it starts as being negotiated); the owners accept or decline on that tab; with Dxcufgb's Quest Log active, accepting adds a side quest to travel there and sign; the GM signs (the setup from the treasury, or waived) and the quest is completed. Unsigned offers lapse after 3 turns (the quest fails). Bigger and farther settlements and rarer goods pay more; caravans can be raided in dangerous land and stop in a siege. Trade routes need a Trading Post: without one nobody asks and none can be sought, and players don't see the tab (the GM does, with a note). Each Trading Post (build up to five) handles three routes and earns 10 GP a turn in fees; the Trading Company handles two more. One settlement can keep several routes at once, more the bigger it is: 3 for a hamlet, 6 a village, 9 a town, 12 a city, 15 a metropolis; the settlements list shows how many each has. Codex pages and paintings for the Trading Post and trade caravans. Hooks `tradeRouteRequested`, `tradeRouteAccepted`, `tradeRouteSigned`, `tradeRouteEnded`.
- Hidden Approach: building a road to the Bastion (Build a Road or Pave the Road) ends it when the road is finished (a trail keeps it), and it can't be planned once there's a road. Planning one while the other is built or planned warns in the catalog and asks first, from the catalog and from the road's upgrade arrow.
- Facilities page: buildings of the same kind share one row (three watchtowers: "Watchtower × 3", their stats added up), and every section folds away by clicking its heading (remembered in this browser).
- Food for barren sites: the Hunters' Guild (no game needed; trappers' and rangers' lodge, great hunt: up to 135 food a day, and pelts sold for 8 GP a turn, up to 45 GP; its hunters are workers), Highland Herds (hills, mountains or barren land; summer pastures, drovers' station) and Mushroom Cellars (food inside the walls anywhere, even underground or in a siege; no room on the grounds). Codex pages and paintings. The Hunting Lodge now also earns 5 GP a turn from hides and furs.
- Food buildings yield half as much food again (every building with a food yield, inside and outside the walls).
- Merchants pay market fees: when a merchant arrives, its stall fees, tolls and trade go into the treasury, by its tier (10 GP a peddler, 30 a caravan, 75 a merchant company, 150 an exotic trader) times the market's (×1 Market Square, ×2 Market Hall, ×4 Market Town); nothing without a market. The turn card shows it, and the Facilities page's Trade and crafting summary gives each visit's fee and the average a turn. House rules `merchantVisitGold`, `merchantMarketMult`.
- Balance: every site, fully built, now earns within about 750 GP a turn of a fully built river keep on the plains (timber, stone, game, a town 10 miles off) and grows at least as much food as it eats, whatever its terrain, land, climate, water, resources, distance or structure. 18 new buildings that only some sites can build make up for what a site lacks, each with a Codex page and painting:
  - food where farms fail: Fungus Farms and Cavern Fish Pools (underground), Oasis Gardens (desert or arid land, with water), Ice Fishers' and Sealers' Camp (cold or arctic), Eel Weirs and Rice Paddies (swamp), Foragers' Lodge (poor or barren land);
  - trade and access: Caravan Depot (no river or coast: Accessibility +3, exotic traders as a caravan town), Deep Road Gate (underground: Accessibility +3, exotic traders), Mountain Pass Toll (hills, mountains), Caravanserai (desert, arid), Fur Trading Lodge (cold, arctic), Peat Cutters (swamp), Crystal Galleries (underground), Horse Breeders' Ranch (plains), Townhouses to Let (urban), Saltworks (coast), Charcoal Burners (forest), Brickworks (no stone nearby; sells its surplus like a Quarry).
- Food: only farms (fields, orchards, pasture) depend on the land and climate; hunting, fishing, herding and foraging now feed a Bastion on barren land too (the Hunters' Guild and Highland Herds gave nothing there before). A forest counts as having timber, and hills, mountains and caves as having stone. Gems make a Mine pay a quarter more (was double; house rule `gemsMult`), so ore and gems lift a Bastion no more than about 650 GP a turn above the River Keep.
- API: `core.fullBuild(location, { rules, catalog, structure })` builds everything a site allows and gives its stats, gold and food; `core.RIVER_KEEP` is the reference site.
- Quarry and Lumber Camp: while no project under way uses stone (or timber), they sell what they cut and fell instead and earn double (house rule `surplusSaleMult`). The Facilities page's Trade and crafting summary says which is selling and which is supplying a project.
- Projects catalog: buildings that can be built here later show locked with what they still need (a building, Accessibility, room) instead of being hidden; buildings the site rules out for good (no ore, no water, no farmland, no game…) are left out and listed apart under "Can't be built here" with the reason. The "one of every building still missing" estimate counts only what can be built here.
- Industries: the Brewery, Merchant Charter, Mine and Quarry expand up to 10 levels. Each level costs more than the last (x1.6) and yields a little more (ale, traders and fees, ore, stone), but needs more workers. Workers live at the Bastion: they need beds and food like everyone else, and are paid 1 GP a turn each in wages (upkeep; the Upkeep column shows each building's). Every building with workers (Brewery, Merchant Charter, Quarry, Mine, Hunters' Guild, Trading Post) earns more than its workers cost in wages and in food bought at the dearest price from its third level on, and more with every level after. The catalog shows long upgrade paths as the first steps, "…" and the last.
- Food: when the stores run dry, the shortfall is bought with the treasury (2 sp a person-day, more on a poor road) before any dividend; the turn card shows the cost. When the treasury can't pay either, people go hungry and those who can't be fed move away: refugees and guests first, then servants, then workers (their industries earn that much less until they come back, half of them each fed turn), then hired guards and mercenaries. Not during a siege.
- More food: the Kitchen Garden (walled garden, terraced gardens), Orchard (fruit groves, cider estate) and Livestock Pens (dairy and piggery, cattle ranch) have upgrade tiers. New: the Fishery (needs a stream, river, lake or coast; fish weirs, fishing fleet) and the Greenhouse (food grown inside the walls, so it counts in a siege; glass gardens, orangery), each with a Codex page and painting.
- The Garrison Hall upgrades to Barracks, a Barracks Ward and a Citadel Garrison: up to 100 hired guards, with beds for them.
- Facilities page: the double arrow plans the upgrade straight away (the next tier, a bigger room, the next road step); it appears in the constructions list on the Projects page. The Projects page no longer lists the buildings already built (the Facilities page does). The Trade and crafting section has a Workers column and shows workers who moved away; Food and water shows what a shortfall would cost a turn.
- Projects: the dnd5e special facilities defined in the world (world items and compendiums) are in the catalog, in their sections. An owner adds one to a character who has the level and a free special facility slot (and not the same one already).
- One of a kind: the Parlor, the Courtyard and the Beacon Tower can be built only once, and no road step can be planned once the road is a paved highway.
- The system's Bastion rules: when they are off in a world, the GMs get one whispered chat card (once per world, instead of a notice at every load) saying the module works without them, with **Turn on** (the system's rules on, its own Bastion turn button kept off: the module runs the turns) and **Keep off** (remembered).
- Opening a Bastion's journal entry (sidebar, links) opens the Bastion page straight away, without the journal window flashing up first.
- Fix: the House rules and catalog window opens again (its template had two root elements).
- Fix: planned projects are re-priced when the Prices or Build pace setting changes and when the world loads (it failed on a missing import).
- Deleting a Bastion clears the Bastion name it had put on the owners' sheets.
- Players no longer get the Bastions scene-control group or the journal sidebar button: their character sheet is the way in. The GM keeps both, to found Bastions and see them all.

## 0.1.0
- Dxcufgb's Debug Recorder: when it is installed, the module registers with it (whatever the load order) and gives it a snapshot of its state (settings, clock, and per Bastion: projects by state, crews, queue, dispatches, map and marker, errors) and breadcrumbs at every action, turn, long rest, finished project and map build while the GM records. Only counts, own ids and document ids: no names, text, image paths or URLs. Without the recorder nothing changes.
- Module id `dxcufgbs-player-bastions` (title "Dxcufgb's Player Bastions"), matching the repo `dxcufgb/FoundryVTT-player-bastions`. Bastions made under the earlier development id `dxcufgbs-player-bastion` aren't carried over.
- Grounds: a Bastion has a size (Plot, Compound, Holding, Fortress, Citadel) that holds a set number of squares of buildings. Each expansion has a footprint; planned projects hold their room. When the grounds are full, nothing more is built or enlarged inside the walls until an "Extend the grounds" project finishes, which clears new land and lengthens every built wall layer. Shown on the Projects and Stats tabs and in the wizard, where the GM can also start a Bastion on bigger grounds.
- Battle map: a "Build battle map" button (GM) on the Projects tab draws the Bastion as its own scene with Foundry walls and doors. A layout engine places each building inside the walls when it is finished and never moves it again (enlarged rooms grow in place where they can); keep rooms form one block, yards and gardens go toward the corners, the gate road stays clear. The map is redrawn after turns that finish a building and when a siege breaches the wall. Every battle map goes in a scene folder called "Bastions" (maps made earlier outside any folder are moved there when the world loads). API: `buildMap`, `viewMap`, `mapPlan`.
- Battle map look, in the manner of modern sourcebook maps: soft sun shadows and ambient occlusion, thick stone walls with doors in their frames, grass baileys worn into dirt paths from every door, clutter along the walls, rugs under the furniture, trees and small details round the edge by terrain, warm firelight (also real Foundry lights at every hearth, forge, brazier and gate torch, lit only once the scene gets dark), and a warm colour grade. 53 pieces of art made with ElevenLabs. Round tower and square gatehouse-tower roofs, a thicker curtain wall, orchards of overlapping fruit trees, and cave Bastions drawn as halls cut into the rock with a tunnel out.
- Build pace: building takes days, not months, by default (a room 2 to 8 days, a great hall 8); a "Build pace" setting picks Adventure, Campaign or DMG times. Unfinished projects are re-timed when the pace changes, keeping the share of work done.
- Long-rest clock: without a calendar module, the party's long rests drive the Bastion: a day of work per rest, a turn every 7 rests, and the page counts in long rests. A "Bastion clock" setting can force either clock; the GM has an "A long rest passed" button.
- Battle map: the first watchtower stands in the middle of the grounds on every map (4 × 4 squares, walls and a door facing the road); more towers go on the corners. Rooms that stood there in an older layout are placed again.
- Projects tab: a summary of what one of every building still missing would take, in gold and long rests (days with a calendar), per group and in all.
- Projects tab: started projects sit at the top (building, then paused), then the ones closest to done: least gold left, then least work left.
- Projects tab: the Funding and Build headers show the totals (gold paid in of the total, work done of the total), and the list of expansions shows only what can be planned right now.
- Battle map road: the location's road type is painted (none, a narrow dirt trail, a dirt road, a cobbled highway) and, until the Bastion has a wall, it just runs on into the grounds; the paved way to the door only comes with a wall.
- Founding: a structure no longer brings a set of rooms. The GM picks ONE first building in the Structure step (cottage, house, large house, hall, tower or chapel; the structure suggests one); anything more goes in the First build step.
- Battle map: with no wall, no barrels, crates or hay stand along the edge, and watchtowers after the centre one stand free near the middle instead of on empty corners.
- Long-rest clock: the Bastion page, the dashboard and the turn card show no dates without a calendar module; merchants leave and dispatches arrive "after N long rests" on the Bastion's own clock.
- Battle map: with no wall the ground runs on unbroken inside the grounds (no seam where the bailey starts).
- Battle map ground: the GM picks the ground the battle map is painted on in the founding or edit wizard (grass, dirt, mud, sand, snow, cobblestone, cave floor, bright rock, or automatic by terrain), whatever the other answers.
- Walls: Bastions start without walls (the keep, castle, manor, temple and fortified tower no longer come with one; the tower's own walls no longer count as a wall round the grounds). There is one Wall: built as a palisade and upgraded in place to stone, then reinforced, then a wall walk with crenellations, each step priced along the whole wall. Existing Bastions' separate Palisade, Stone Wall, Reinforce and Wall Walk fold into it at the highest step built.
- Spike Barricade: a one-time ring of stakes at the foot of the wall, outside it and inside any ditch or moat (defense x1.05, light engines -10 %), drawn on the battle map and lengthened with the wall.
- Extending the grounds takes longer (and costs more) the more the wall has been upgraded: every step of the wall per new square, the spike barricade, and digging the ditch or moat out to the new line.
- Battle map: buttresses on a reinforced wall, crenellations with the wall walk, a timber gate in a wall without a gatehouse, a bridge over any ditch or moat.
- Trade: every ware opens its item page from the compendium.
- Delete Bastion (GM): a red button on the Overview tab; the dialog says it is permanent and the GM must type DELETE. It also removes the world-map marker, the battle map scene and the room facilities the Bastion added to the characters.
- Projects tab: each project's buttons sit in a row along the bottom of its line, spaced apart.
- "Finish now" (GM): a button on each project finishes it at once, whatever its funding and progress; "Finish all" in the Build column header finishes every unfinished project.
- Scene controls: the Bastions have a control group of their own (after Journal Notes) instead of a button under Notes: Bastion list, Open my Bastion (players with a Bastion) and Found a Bastion (GM). The group has its own (empty) canvas layer, so clicking it opens the Bastion tools instead of falling back to the Token controls.
- House rules window: it scrolls now (Save stays at the bottom), shows the rules in use and the default catalog besides the default rules, and downloads the default rules, rules in use, default catalog, catalog in use and your overrides as JSON files; "Load a file" fills either box from a JSON file.
- Prices: a "Prices" setting with three models, Fair (default; about 12% off a Cramped room up to 23% off a 7,500 GP work), Thrifty (19% to 39% off) and DMG. Small jobs keep their price; crews cost 40 GP and 8 GP a turn at Fair (25 / 5 Thrifty, 50 / 10 DMG). Planned projects are re-priced when the setting changes, and on the first load (they were planned at the DMG's prices); gold paid beyond a new price goes into the treasury. The catalog's room prices follow the model too.
- Bastion list: an open list updates at once when a Bastion is founded or deleted (on every client), and a deleted Bastion's page closes.
- Build queue: the party (any owner, not only the GM) puts planned projects in a queue and orders it (up / down / out of the queue); the next queued project starts the moment a crew comes free, also when the GM finishes one with Finish now. Running projects sit at the top of the list, then the queue in its order ("Queued #1" ...), then the other plans. A project sent back to the queue for want of crews goes to its head.
- Projects tab: a building with an enlarge, upgrade or repair project planned or under way shows that project instead of its Enlarge / Upgrade / Repair buttons. "Approved" projects are now called "Queued" (waiting for a crew).
- Battle map: redrawn whenever a project finishes (a turn, a long rest, Finish now or Finish all), not only when a new building joins the layout; several finishes in a row make one redraw.
- Building crews: a Bastion starts with one crew and builds one project at a time; the party hires more (Projects tab: hire and dismiss, 50 GP and 10 GP upkeep a turn each) and every Workers' Lodge adds one. A counter shows the crews at work of the total. Projects can't start, resume or take more crews than are free; a queued project starts by itself when a crew comes free, also part-way through a turn. More crews on one project no longer cost extra (house rule `extraCrewCostPct`), and the picker offers as many crews as the Bastion has. The Workers' Lodge now adds a crew to the Bastion instead of a crew to every project. Older Bastions with more projects running than crews send the ones started last back to the queue at the next day of work.
- Fix: the "Set crews" button on a project said "Unknown action"; it now sets the extra crews. Crews can only change before building starts or while paused, and the page says so otherwise.
- Fix: changing the crews on a project already begun lost work: 2 of 8 days done, then three crews, showed 0 of 3. The share of the work still to do now stays the same and the days left round down (2 of 3 left, so 1 of 3 done); an unfinished job keeps at least one day left. A new build pace re-times projects the same way.
- Fix: a finished "Extend the grounds" project now keeps the new size (the turn didn't save it).
- First build: founding wizard with a map marker and location questions, Bastion page (overview, stats, defense, facilities, projects, dispatches, trade, history), GM dashboard.
- Stats with tangible numbers and the siege holdout model, a live siege.
- Expansion catalog of 71 (rooms and walls at DMG prices), tiered revenue buildings, treasury, upkeep and party dividends.
- Projects: players plan and fund, the GM approves, starts, pauses and cancels; crews and rush.
- Dispatches: envoy, letter, messenger bird, familiar, Animal Messenger, Sending, Sending Stones, Dream, beacons, scrying; standing orders with a Steward's Office.
- Bastion turns around the dnd5e system's own turn, all 11 DMG Bastion events, merchants and visitors.
- Self-tests: engine tests (Node and Quench) and in-Foundry integration tests (Quench).
- Art: a sourcebook-style painting for each of the ten terrains and four sites; the GM picks the Bastion's picture in the wizard (terrain, site or any image).
- Fixes from the first test run in Foundry:
  - Tabs on the Bastion page switch when clicked (the "tab" action name clashed with Foundry's own).
  - Choosing an expansion for the first build no longer resets before it is added.
  - Typing in one dispatch field no longer loses the text of the next one.
  - A Bastion turn happens at its due date, so a dispatch arriving during the week is delivered; missed turns catch up one week at a time.
  - The "one more turn without orders" warning no longer shows on a turn that had orders.
  - Wares under 1 GP show in SP or CP and cost their real price; the buyer gets change.
  - Hiring never dismisses the guards or servants the Bastion was founded with.
  - The edit wizard previews the Bastion's real stats.
  - Party members' character sheets get a rook button that opens the Bastion.
  - Dates read like "January 8th, 16:20"; "1 day" instead of "1 days".
  - A notice tells the GM when the system's own Bastion rules are turned off in the world.
